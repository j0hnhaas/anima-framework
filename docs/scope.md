# Scope and definitions

This document defines what ANIMA covers. The model every source is mapped onto is described in [model/README.md](../model/README.md); all codes are listed in [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md).

## 1. Subject

ANIMA covers two fields:

1. **Psychology as a profession** – psychologists who use AI in their work, or whose clients use AI. This includes clinical and health psychology as well as other fields of psychological work, such as psychological assessment, expert opinions, work, school and traffic psychology.
2. **Psychological effects of AI** – how AI systems affect people's experience and behaviour, for example trust, dependence, wellbeing, self-image or decisions. This includes the use of AI **without any practitioner**, such as general-purpose chatbots or wellness apps.

Both fields meet in one model: the triad of client, practitioner and AI system. Psychological effects can occur at every edge of the triad – for the client through the AI system (C–AI), for the practitioner through the AI system (P–AI), and in the relationship between client and practitioner (C–P).

### Boundary to psychotherapy and psychiatry

Psychotherapy and psychiatry are **not covered as professions**: their professional law, their professional bodies and their specific duties are out of scope.

Their **settings** are covered where psychological effects of AI are concerned. Example: a person in psychotherapy who uses a chatbot between sessions experiences psychological effects of AI. These effects are in scope; the psychotherapist's professional duties are not.

## 2. Sources

ANIMA organises its sources in **pillars**. Each source also has a **jurisdiction** and a degree of **bindingness**.

| Pillar | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Law** | What is binding? | EU regulations, national laws, binding decisions of authorities |
| **Guidance** | What do public bodies recommend or how do they interpret the law? | WHO, WHO Europe, European Commission guidelines, national health authorities and HTA bodies (e.g. NICE) |
| **Professional** | What do professional bodies require of their members or recommend? | EFPA, American Psychological Association (APA), national associations of psychologists |
| **Evidence** *(planned)* | What does research show? | studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses |

**Jurisdiction** states where a source applies or who issued it: global, pan-European, EU, or a single country. Sources from outside Europe (e.g. from the United Kingdom or the United States) are included as **international references** for good practice. They are not binding in the EU.

**Evidence** is a separate pillar, not the lowest level of bindingness: a recommendation can be strongly supported by evidence without being binding, and a binding rule can lack evidence. The evidence pillar will be added in a later version.

## 3. Spectrum

Many AI applications in psychology lie on a spectrum between wellbeing and treatment. Where an application sits often decides which rules apply.

| Stage | Description | Typical regulatory question |
|---|---|---|
| **Wellbeing** | stress reduction, mindfulness, mood tracking | Is this (still) not a medical device? |
| **Prevention** | psychoeducation, early detection, screening | Where does a medical purpose begin? |
| **Diagnostics and treatment** | psychological assessment, diagnosis, treatment | Medical device, high-risk AI system, professional law |

The boundary between wellbeing and a medical purpose often depends on the intended purpose a provider claims, including marketing claims.

## 4. Definitions

**Psychology.** The science of human experience and behaviour and the profession based on it. In ANIMA, psychology as a profession means the work of psychologists (see *practitioner*).

**Psychological effects of AI.** Effects of AI systems on people's experience and behaviour, whether intended or not, and whether or not a practitioner is involved.

**Client, practitioner, AI system.** The three nodes of the triad, defined in the [model](../model/README.md#1-nodes). In short: the *client* is the person with or about whom psychological work is done, or who is psychologically affected by an AI system; the *practitioner* is a psychologist who carries professional responsibility; the *AI system* is defined as in Art. 3(1) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

**Mental health.** ANIMA follows the broad WHO understanding: mental health is a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with stress, realise their abilities and take part in their community. It is more than the absence of mental disorders. Source: WHO, *Mental health: strengthening our response* (fact sheet).

## 5. Out of scope

- Psychotherapy and psychiatry as professions (see section 1)
- Product reviews or recommendations of specific AI tools or providers
- Technical standards (e.g. ISO/IEC, CEN-CENELEC) beyond references
- Countries without a contributor who can check the national sources (open for contributions)
