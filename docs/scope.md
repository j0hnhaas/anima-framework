# Scope and definitions

This document defines what ANIMA covers and how requirements are classified.

## 1. Regulatory layers

Requirements are organised in four layers, from global recommendations to national law. The further down, the more specific the requirements become; from the EU layer on, they are legally binding.

| # | Layer | Nature | Folder |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | WHO global | Recommendations, ethical principles | [`01-who-global/`](../01-who-global/) |
| 2 | WHO Europe | Regional recommendations and action plans | [`02-who-europe/`](../02-who-europe/) |
| 3 | European Union | Binding law (regulations, directives) and guidance | [`03-eu/`](../03-eu/) |
| 4 | National | Binding national law and professional rules | [`04-national/`](../04-national/) |

WHO guidance is not legally binding, but it shapes good practice and is often cited by regulators. EU and national law are binding.

## 2. Usage contexts

| Context | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Professional-led** | A professional uses AI in their own work. | AI-assisted documentation, test scoring, literature research |
| **Professional-supervised** | Clients use AI as part of, or alongside, a treatment. | App-based exercises between therapy sessions |
| **Self-directed** | People use AI on their own, without a professional. | General-purpose chatbots for emotional support, wellness apps |

Self-directed use is explicitly in scope. It is probably the most common use case, and often the least regulated.

## 3. Spectrum

| Stage | Description | Typical regulatory question |
|---|---|---|
| **Wellbeing** | Stress reduction, mindfulness, mood tracking | Is this (still) not a medical device? |
| **Prevention** | Early detection, psychoeducation, risk screening | Where does a medical purpose begin? |
| **Diagnostics and treatment** | Assessment, diagnosis, therapy | Medical device, high-risk AI, professional law |

The boundary between wellbeing and medical purpose often depends on the intended purpose a provider claims, including marketing claims.

## 4. Key definitions

**Mental health.** ANIMA follows the broad WHO understanding: mental health is a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with stress, realise their abilities and take part in their community. It is more than the absence of mental disorders. Source: WHO, *Mental health: strengthening our response* (fact sheet).

**Psychology.** Includes clinical and health psychology and psychotherapy. Other fields (e.g. work, school or traffic psychology) may be added later.

**AI system.** As defined in Art. 3(1) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

All codes used for layers, levels, usage contexts, spectrum and topics are defined in [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md).

## 5. Out of scope (for now)

- Countries other than Austria at the national layer (open for contributions)
- Technical standards (e.g. ISO/IEC, CEN-CENELEC) beyond references
- Product reviews or recommendations of specific AI tools
